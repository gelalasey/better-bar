## The Set Up 

I'm not going to delve too much into what [SASS](https://xixxii.neocities.org/projects/suckatsomethingseptember) is because they've already created a website to do that for me. Let's go delegation. I'm also not going to excuse myself at any point by making the excuse that I was really busy starting a new job and moving this month to be able to participate properly. I have adhd, my neighbor mowing his lawn too early can get me off track. I committed to this experiment and whatever I created is my own fault/accomplishment. What I was trying to create was a """""simple""""" (we will delve into this term more) recipe app. I currently use Notion to hold all my recipes, but as the search bar got replaced with an AI chat my frustration with the platform has been steadily rising to a boil. I've since swapped over to Obsidian, but the set up isn't exactly what I wanted for mealtime. I envisioned something more dynamic. 

I wanted to keep a lot of the same meta data from Notion. I loved being able to search for recipes by ingredient or seasonality. I liked being able to assign each recipe a day and plot out my schedule of when I would make it. I wanted to create a system that both held all of my .md files as well as allowing me to create one. Having just gotten the knack of setting up my Obisdian, I thought I knew enough to be able to make this.

## Deceptively Simple 

I dont think I'm an overly full of myself guy, but I think we are all more prone to a self-assurance bias than we realize we are. I thought I knew enough to be able to build this app. I knew html and css. I started working with Javascript related prompts. I thought I certainly wasn't going to build anything incredible, but I really believed I had the basic skills to figure this out with office hours help from Professors Google and Youtube. Maybe even utilizing the services of AI TA Co-Pilot or Gemini if super desparate. The funny thing about the internet is that we have access to more information that we ever have had in any civilization. Artificial intelligence creates quick solutions to problems. But what all these tools do not do is make us better at problem solving or learning. I know... really bold of me to be so anti new web here on Neocities. I'm not preaching to the choir to get comments cheering me on, rather what I realized is even I, the walking middle finger to big tech am still vastly unaware of all that it is that I don't know. Including, the words to even describe what it was I wanted to do. 

## What the fuck am I building

We know what an app is. We know what the internet is. But beyond that there is apparently a lot of other words that developers know that I don't. The frustration of even finding a tutorial was hurdle number one. I knew I wanted to build a recipe app. But I didn't want to use AI so that took out like...a good 60% of the tutorials I found at first search. You might think the next step would be just to write "no ai" but apparently the key word search triggers the algorithm to serve you even MORE AI based tutorials. So I had to reverse search my way to figure out what exactly I was building. Was it front end or back end? What the hell is a 'CRUD' system? I tell the search bar I want to use Javascript, it comes back at me like "okay that's cute, do you plan to use a mouse and keyboard as well"? Javascript was not the be all end all language I had thought it was. It was more like knowing base loan words from english that all other languages use like "smart phone" or "bobba tea" and expecting that to let you speak fluent Russian. Turns out there are a lot of things called APIs which is the term for all the other plugins and dodads that allow software systems to interact and exchange data. So if you want to build a recipe app and think you'll just be using javascript, newsflash, you're going to have to use at least 40 other of these API guys to get anything to do anything other than "hello world". 

## Tutorial Hell 
Alright I got some of my key words. This is where I hilariously thought I had learned my lesson. *LOL SASS is all about failing, guess I'm going to make something a lot more primative than I thought I would. Hehe* Past Gelsey... you have no idea how absolutely rocked your socks are about to become. 

In theory, anyone should be able to do anything with a proper tutorial. Coding is just typing lines into a file. In theory if my computer does the same thing that your computer does we both have created the same thing. Robot to robot there should be no room for error. Only guess what that is a LIE. Systems update all the damn time and what worked for someone 5 months ago could infact include obsolete components by the time you try to build. "No problem" you think to yourself "I'll just look up what the modern best practice is and do that line instead" only now your single out of place domino has sent forth a tidal wave of bug errors until your simple button can no longer click without risk of deleting your entire harddrive some how. That's an exaggeration for dramatic effect but I can't confirm I didn't almost do that. The problem with following a tutorial is that when you have no idea what you are doing you are basically doing a trust walk, you are allowing the instructor to guide you either across the room or over a boiling pit of lava on a tightrope. And if you're blindfolded you may have no idea which scenario you are currently in. I am so lucky that I didn't end up breaking anything other than project folders, but I wont pretend I was fully aware that was all I was risking the entire time.

## What the hell did we learn from all this?

1. Babier Baby Steps: Was this way more fun to build and learn than an app? YES, because the threshold of the unknown was so much lower. I realized that you can have big goals and ambitions but in order to reach that flow state you have to take the journey one step at a time. And that doesn't mean **tutorial step** that means getting comfortable and knowing what you're doing with the tools in hand. You can tell me to tap a nail 5 times with a hammer, but if I don't know the purpose of that hammer is to make the nail go into the wood then I won't ever be able to do anything other than tap that nail 5 times. Could I do more taps? Less? I won't know until I learn what a hammer is for. I need to learn that and then go off and put a bunch of nails into wood. Spell my name out in nails. Hammer into different surfaces and see what happens. Where is this anaology going again?

2. Play to Learn: You do not learn by following directions. You learn by grasping a concept and creating with it. I was so hell bent on trying to create this outcome that I didn't give myself the freedom to play. Not only is it essential to learning, but it's the only way to have fun with what you're doing. It's why I learned so much Japanese after I used it to start translating Uma Musume songs back in the day. I had a dopamine fueled need and it pushed me all the way with fun power. 

3. Create to refuel yourself: Following directions on a detail oriented tutorial was not how I wanted to spend my freetime. I wanted to make stories, make art, doodle little things in the margins. That's not only what motivates me, but it's what gives me energy to do the shitty stuff I have to do on a daily basis. If I want to be able to create anything through web dev I need to think like an artist first because that's what I am. Despite wishing I could be a cool bald headed hacker of justice like Lizbet Salander, I am in fact just a neurodivergent clown with an addiction to novelty and lexapro. And that's okay. ...Well not the lexapro thing, but like the accepting myself as who I am thing. Bad bitches really can do anything, but they also have to realize sometimes that their path to success and their dreams isn't like every other bad bitch. 

4. SOMEONE ELSE ALREADY BUILT VERSIONS OF MY TTRPG: It's actually a really common theme in the self care world. But if I end up following through on this project I at least know there's an audience for it. I'm coming for your bag Habbitica just watch me.
