I was inspired by channels like Studson Studio making the [BOTW horse stable](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBT_fI-D5mk) or watching hanabira workshop build a [whimsical magic forest in their computer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-Y2FVB0nj_Q). The thing about these videos is that they make it look so easy to create an entire miniature world. All you need is a bit of junk, some glue, and creativity. Then **bam** your own personalized dollhouse is complete. But what they fail to mention in these videos is how they’ve developed the talent to build and make what they do. And like all hobbies, you have to start out at your worst before you ascend to your best. 

I want to go over my own first build - the little cupcake kitchen and talk about the lessons I’ve learned in my own builds. In only 3 builds I’ve already learned so much and as I continue to make more miniatures I hope to continue to see my own growth. I’m not trying to bash myself - but more so share some things I could have only learned by trying and failing. I hope this inspires anyone else who may have had a lackluster first attempt at their first build to keep trying and enjoy the process of learning 

## Build Concept 
For my first build I think the initial inspiration came from the set of mini toys that I had, a little cupcake making set with a bowl, tray and teenie tiny little eggs. The bright colors and faces on all the equipment made it clear that if I was going to build I had to do it with all the kidcore aesthetics. Bright colors, interactive elements and not taking it too seriously. 
I decided to do a smaller kitchenette so i wouldn’t be completely overwhelmed. The concept was simple, two countertops, 1 cabinet, 1 oven. Perfect for building a little station to make cupcakes. I kept the ratio at a little smaller than 1:6. I proportioned everything for my Licca chan doll to be able to bake easily. Which leads me into lesson 1. 

## SUCCESS: Have a Ratio Guide Nearby
When you’re doing a freebuild and making your own furniture it helps to have a doll, figure or otherwise nearby to make sure your ratios make sense. You don’t want to have countertops coming up to an adult's chin but bowls so small they look like coffee cups. Having Licca oversee the build process was a good check when I didn’t have a straight ratio. I would just look at how objects were sized compared to my people's body and tried to create the same for her doll body.

## FLOP: Beware Cardboard 
So my first real mistake with this build was trying to make the base out of a cardboard snack box. Cardboard is meant to be a temporary holder. It is very sensitive to moisture and doesn’t have as much structural integrity. This flexibility is great for certain materials, it doesn’t make for a good solid floor and wall for a house.

![](https://64.media.tumblr.com/b988c0b4f3c9cf72a8f5a252b571d140/4ccbaaeb5b211621-41/s2048x3072/480318004c798d3d3f07447991cb8cf1b7685653.jpg)

I ended up having to reinforce my cardboard with more and more layers. And more layers meant more glue, and more glue meant more warping from liquid. For a square room nothing beats good old fashioned wood imo. But if you are going with an upcycled project like mine was, try to find more solid material like the box your new phone comes in. You want something at least .5cm thick. 

## SUCCESS: Measure 70x
My flooring is one of the better parts of this build. I watched a lot of Hanabira cutting popsicle sticks to understand that this was something that would take up the most time of the build. I measured each section of stick very carefully to make sure things would be as inline as possible. And then I used sandpaper to file down parts that were not quite even. 

![](https://64.media.tumblr.com/505efee205d79bf97fbc087824e25548/4ccbaaeb5b211621-3f/s2048x3072/a0647b7717a5cd29f9ea9ffdb0d75db6d944d16f.jpg)
*Can you tell which floor my aderall ran out on?*

Good builds need a good foundation and rushing the cutting and measuring is an easy way to end up with a sloppy build. Pencils are your friend. Always make back ups. And enjoy the slow detain focused process of making each piece fit perfectly into its puzzle. 

## FLOP: keep movement in mind 
So I totally understood how ot make my floor even (despite gluing it down a little wonky). The less elegant build was my counter top. The frame itself I’m alright with - but my attempt to make an oven and cupboard that could open on the other hand…

![](https://64.media.tumblr.com/4c288841a9830c17eeb4f7b88e7f6b91/4ccbaaeb5b211621-fd/s2048x3072/f05bc251f1973e1c76ea226b3bd273ef23c5de3a.jpg)
*whomp whomp whomp whommmmmmp*

I didn’t realize that the dimensions would have to work both when they were open and closed. Look at your own cabinets and doors in the house. Look at the trim around that makes them seal properly. Look at how hinges are installed. Observe the gaps between pieces. That’s what I’d do next time moving forward. Also maybe get some real hinges, in my case I just poked a paperclip through the foam board which probably also didn’t help its structural integrity.

![](https://64.media.tumblr.com/bee91210dbb1ebfb2daf10e0c0ddb5d2/4ccbaaeb5b211621-d3/s2048x3072/500034cdf279ee83b65b4a9c801efc8af53e0523.jpg)

Had I really looked at my own oven I probably would have been able to mirror its method of opening for an easier use. 

## SUCCESS hiding little details
The best part of a miniature is really bringing the world to life with all the small parts. Not everything needs to be noticed immediately, the goal is that the more someone looks at your build, the more they see. I was really happy with the tiny post-its I left around the countertops that were almost too tricky to write at the size. Or the little grey cloud sticker hidden behind the curtains. If I could rebuild this I might have added some lighting effects in the window to brighten it up even more. 

![](https://64.media.tumblr.com/08dee006b38185b4a4a5177f48f5d378/4ccbaaeb5b211621-3e/s2048x3072/2e2be961a019673b1ac31546eea3a49693aa58bc.jpg)

## FLOP: The Dust Factor 
So you may have noticed in a lot of these pictures some… grossness. This build is covered in dust and cat hair after years of sitting on my shelf. Part of it is just natural, you will get dust that accumulates in your builds and that’s why you need to be sure to dust them regularly.

![](https://64.media.tumblr.com/931730c6530da35cf7198af3d49d8264/4ccbaaeb5b211621-ee/s2048x3072/81d0411f45c21fc5bb02d0d7260c9bc2c2d2d544.jpg)

But in my build’s case certain material wouldn't just attract the dust, it would make it CLING. The spilt cake batter was a cute idea until the little glitter flakes would make cat hair cling to the floor giving it a messy look. The teenie gravel cardstock that seemed so perfect for a countertop ended up being a blackhole for tiny specs of dirt. OF course this doesn’t mean I’d recommend avoiding these materials,but consider getting a dust coverso your build doesn’t get icky like mine did… 

## SUCCESS: Finding innovative build material 
For my first junk build I had a lot of fun finding random items I could use to turn into my kitchen. A random poker chip became a clock after I slapped a sticker on it. A gift tag was folded to become a recipe folder. Don’t underestimate the power of the random junk you have around your home! 


## FLOP: Giving up!
Thank god I didn't do this - but whatever you do - don't give up. Finish the project even with its cracks, bends and crooked rows. It was worth it to finish something and see a final product even if it could have been done better. You can always do better like shit man. You'll never be ready enough or perfect enough. Just make something. Maybe you'll hate it, maybe you'll end up loving your mistakes the most! Maybe you'll go fully insane and eat your mini build. Maybe you'll get poisoned from eatting the build, meet a hot doctor at the ER, fall in love, get married, have children, and they one day will say "dang I wish I had a mini dollhouse to play with. Too bad my parent gave up on miniature building". Life is unpredictable man. Just glue some popsicle sticks together and be happy.
